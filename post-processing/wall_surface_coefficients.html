Wall surface coefficients¶

The flow around an airfoil acts on the surface in two ways: through the pressure $p$, which pushes normal to the wall, and through the wall shear stress $\tau_w$, which drags tangentially along it. Written in non-dimensional form, these give the two wall surface coefficients:

  • the pressure coefficient $C_p$, and
  • the skin-friction coefficient $C_f$.

Their distributions along the chord show where the airfoil generates lift, where the boundary layer undergoes transition, and where it is close to separating. Integrated around the surface, they give the lift and drag coefficients.

This notebook defines both coefficients, computes them for a NACA 0012 airfoil with XFOIL, and checks that integrating them reproduces the airfoil loads.

1. Problem setup¶

A 2D airfoil with chord $c$ sits in a uniform freestream of speed $U_\infty$, pressure $p_\infty$ and density $\rho_\infty$, at an angle of attack $\alpha$ between the freestream and the chord line.

At each point on the surface we use two unit vectors:

  • $\hat{\mathbf n}$, the outward normal, and
  • $\hat{\mathbf t}$, the tangent pointing in the local flow direction, away from the stagnation point.

The pressure acts along $-\hat{\mathbf n}$ (into the wall), and the wall shear stress acts along $\hat{\mathbf t}$. Adding them up over the whole surface gives the resultant force, which is split into lift $L$ (normal to the freestream) and drag $D$ (parallel to it).

2. Pressure coefficient $C_p$¶

The pressure coefficient measures the local surface pressure relative to the freestream pressure, scaled by the freestream dynamic pressure $q_\infty$:

$$ C_p = \frac{p - p_\infty}{q_\infty}, \qquad q_\infty = \tfrac12 \rho_\infty U_\infty^2 . $$

For incompressible flow, Bernoulli's equation along the edge of the boundary layer relates $C_p$ to the local edge velocity $U_e$:

$$ C_p = 1 - \left(\frac{U_e}{U_\infty}\right)^2 . $$

This tells us how to read a $C_p$ plot:

Location Edge velocity $C_p$
Stagnation point (near the leading edge) $U_e = 0$ $C_p = 1$
Flow faster than freestream (suction side) $U_e > U_\infty$ $C_p < 0$
Flow slower than freestream $U_e < U_\infty$ $0 < C_p < 1$

By convention, $C_p$ plots use an inverted vertical axis, so the suction side appears on top. The area enclosed between the upper- and lower-surface curves is, to a good approximation, the lift.

The slope of $C_p$ along the surface is the pressure gradient that the boundary layer feels:

  • $\mathrm dC_p/\mathrm dx < 0$, favorable gradient (accelerating flow): the boundary layer stays thin and laminar for longer.
  • $\mathrm dC_p/\mathrm dx > 0$, adverse gradient (decelerating flow): the boundary layer thickens, triggers transition, and can eventually separate.

3. Skin-friction coefficient $C_f$¶

The wall shear stress is the viscous stress at the wall, set by the velocity gradient normal to the wall:

$$ \tau_w = \mu \left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\right|_{n=0}, $$

where $u$ is the velocity component tangential to the wall and $n$ the wall-normal distance. Scaled by the same dynamic pressure,

$$ C_f = \frac{\tau_w}{q_\infty} = \frac{\tau_w}{\tfrac12 \rho_\infty U_\infty^2}. $$

The sign of $C_f$ tells us whether the flow near the wall is attached:

  • $C_f > 0$: the near-wall flow moves in the direction of $\hat{\mathbf t}$ and the boundary layer is attached.
  • $C_f = 0$: the velocity gradient at the wall vanishes. This is the separation (or reattachment) point.
  • $C_f < 0$: reversed flow near the wall, i.e. a separated region or separation bubble.

Flat-plate reference values¶

For a flat plate at zero pressure gradient, $C_f$ depends only on the local Reynolds number $Re_x = U_\infty x/\nu$, where $x$ is the distance from the leading edge:

$$ C_f^{\text{lam}} = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{Re_x}} \quad\text{(Blasius, laminar)}, \qquad C_f^{\text{turb}} = \frac{0.0592}{Re_x^{1/5}} \quad\text{(turbulent, } 5\times10^5 < Re_x < 10^7\text{)}. $$

On an airfoil, the pressure gradient shifts $C_f$ away from these curves. They remain a useful reference, though: the laminar part of the boundary layer should sit near the Blasius curve, and after transition $C_f$ should jump up towards the turbulent curve. Below, $x$ is replaced by the arc length $s$ measured from the stagnation point.

4. From surface coefficients to lift and drag¶

Per unit span, a surface element of length $\mathrm ds$ feels the force $\mathrm d\mathbf F = (-p\,\hat{\mathbf n} + \tau_w\,\hat{\mathbf t})\,\mathrm ds$. A constant pressure exerts no net force on a closed body ($\oint p_\infty \hat{\mathbf n}\,\mathrm ds = 0$), so $p$ can be replaced by $p - p_\infty$. Dividing by $q_\infty c$ gives the force coefficient vector:

$$ \mathbf C_F = \frac{1}{c}\oint \left(-C_p\,\hat{\mathbf n} + C_f\,\hat{\mathbf t}\right)\mathrm ds . $$

Projecting onto the drag direction $\hat{\mathbf e}_D = (\cos\alpha, \sin\alpha)$ and the lift direction $\hat{\mathbf e}_L = (-\sin\alpha, \cos\alpha)$:

$$ C_l = \mathbf C_F\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_L, \qquad C_d = \underbrace{-\frac1c\oint C_p\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_D\,\mathrm ds}_{C_{d,p}\ \text{(pressure drag)}} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac1c\oint C_f\,\hat{\mathbf t}\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_D\,\mathrm ds}_{C_{d,f}\ \text{(friction drag)}} . $$

For a streamlined airfoil at a moderate angle of attack, lift comes almost entirely from $C_p$, and drag is dominated by skin friction.

5. Computing $C_p$ and $C_f$ with XFOIL¶

XFOIL couples an inviscid panel method with an integral boundary-layer model, and predicts transition with the $e^N$ method. For each operating point it returns surface distributions of $C_p$, $U_e/U_\infty$, $C_f$ and the boundary-layer thicknesses.

The XFOIL binary is expected at ../Xfoil/bin_nofpe/xfoil, relative to this notebook. This copy was compiled without floating-point traps, so XFOIL can run with its graphics disabled.

Running XFOIL¶

XFOIL is driven through its command prompt. Each run happens in a temporary directory, which is deleted afterwards, so no output files are left next to the notebook. Two files are read back:

  • CPWR: surface $C_p(x)$
  • DUMP: surface and wake boundary-layer data, including $s$, $x$, $y$, $U_e/U_\infty$ and $C_f$
In [4]:
import os, re, shutil, subprocess, tempfile

def run_xfoil(airfoil, re_num, alpha, mach=0.0, ncrit=9.0, xfoil_bin=XFOIL_BIN):
    xfoil_bin = os.path.abspath(xfoil_bin)
    with tempfile.TemporaryDirectory() as tmp:
        if airfoil.upper().startswith("NACA"):
            load = f"NACA {airfoil.split()[-1]}"
        else:
            shutil.copy(airfoil, os.path.join(tmp, "airfoil.dat"))
            load = "LOAD airfoil.dat"

        cmds = "\n".join([
            "PLOP", "G", "",               # disable graphics
            load, "PANE",                  # load and repanel the airfoil
            "OPER",
            "VPAR", f"N {ncrit}", "",
            f"VISC {re_num}", f"MACH {mach}", "ITER 200",
            f"ALFA {alpha}",
            "CPWR cp.dat", "DUMP bl.dat",
            "", "QUIT", ""])

        env = {k: v for k, v in os.environ.items() if k != "DISPLAY"}
        out = subprocess.run([xfoil_bin], input=cmds, capture_output=True,
                             text=True, cwd=tmp, env=env).stdout
        if "Convergence failed" in out or not os.path.exists(os.path.join(tmp, "bl.dat")):
            raise RuntimeError("XFOIL did not converge for this operating point")

        cp = np.loadtxt(os.path.join(tmp, "cp.dat"), comments="#")
        with open(os.path.join(tmp, "bl.dat")) as f:
            rows = [l.split() for l in f if not l.startswith("#")]

    # Airfoil-surface rows have 12 columns; the wake rows that follow have fewer
    bl = np.array([[float(v) for v in r] for r in rows if len(r) == 12])

    def last(pattern):
        return float(re.findall(pattern, out)[-1])

    return dict(
        s=bl[:, 0], x=bl[:, 1], y=bl[:, 2], ue=bl[:, 3], cf=bl[:, 6], cp=cp[:, 1],
        CL=last(r"CL =\s*(-?[\d.]+)"),  CD=last(r"CD =\s*(-?[\d.]+)"),
        CDf=last(r"CDf =\s*(-?[\d.]+)"), CDp=last(r"CDp =\s*(-?[\d.]+)"),
        xtr_upper=last(r"Side 1 .*?transition at x/c =\s*([\d.]+)"),
        xtr_lower=last(r"Side 2 .*?transition at x/c =\s*([\d.]+)"),
    )

res = run_xfoil(AIRFOIL, RE, ALPHA, MACH, NCRIT)
print(f"{AIRFOIL}, Re = {RE:.1e}, alpha = {ALPHA} deg")
print(f"CL = {res['CL']:.4f}   CD = {res['CD']:.5f}  (CDf = {res['CDf']:.5f}, CDp = {res['CDp']:.5f})")
print(f"Transition: upper x/c = {res['xtr_upper']:.3f}, lower x/c = {res['xtr_lower']:.3f}")

Splitting the surface at the stagnation point¶

XFOIL orders the surface points from the trailing edge, over the upper surface to the leading edge, and back along the lower surface to the trailing edge. It stores $U_e/U_\infty$ with a sign: positive on the upper branch and negative on the lower one. The stagnation point is therefore where $U_e$ changes sign, which is generally not exactly at the leading edge.

Splitting there gives the upper and lower surfaces, the local flow direction $\hat{\mathbf t}$ on each, and the arc length $s$ from the stagnation point used by the flat-plate references.

x, y, ue, cp, cf, s = (res[k] for k in ("x", "y", "ue", "cp", "cf", "s"))

i0 = np.argmax(ue < 0)                       # first point on the lower branch
w = ue[i0 - 1]/(ue[i0 - 1] - ue[i0])         # linear interpolation to Ue = 0
s_stag = s[i0 - 1] + w*(s[i0] - s[i0 - 1])
x_stag = x[i0 - 1] + w*(x[i0] - x[i0 - 1])
y_stag = y[i0 - 1] + w*(y[i0] - y[i0 - 1])

upper = np.arange(i0)[::-1]                  # stagnation point -> TE
lower = np.arange(i0, len(x))
s_from_stag = np.abs(s - s_stag)

print(f"Stagnation point at x/c = {x_stag:.4f}, y/c = {y_stag:.4f}")

6. Integrating the surface coefficients¶

The surface integrals from Section 4 are evaluated panel by panel. Each pair of neighbouring points forms a straight panel with $\mathrm d\mathbf s = (\Delta x, \Delta y)$, and the coefficients are averaged over the panel. Because the contour runs counter-clockwise, the outward normal times the panel length is $\hat{\mathbf n}\,\mathrm ds = (\Delta y, -\Delta x)$. The tangent $\hat{\mathbf t}$ points away from the stagnation point, which on the upper branch is opposite to the point ordering.

In [6]:
a = np.radians(ALPHA)
eD = np.array([np.cos(a), np.sin(a)])
eL = np.array([-np.sin(a), np.cos(a)])

dx, dy = np.diff(x), np.diff(y)
cp_m = 0.5*(cp[1:] + cp[:-1])
cf_m = 0.5*(cf[1:] + cf[:-1])
flow_dir = np.where(np.arange(len(dx)) < i0 - 1, -1.0, 1.0)   # upper panels: flow against point order
# The panel straddling the stagnation point carries almost no shear; it is assigned to the lower side

F_p = np.array([np.sum(-cp_m*dy), np.sum(cp_m*dx)])            # -∮ Cp n ds
F_f = np.array([np.sum(cf_m*flow_dir*dx), np.sum(cf_m*flow_dir*dy)])  # ∮ Cf t ds

CL_int  = (F_p + F_f) @ eL
CDp_int = F_p @ eD
CDf_int = F_f @ eD

Lift and friction drag agree with XFOIL to within a fraction of a percent, which confirms the definitions and the integration.

Pressure drag does not match as closely, and this is expected. XFOIL does not compute $C_d$ from the surface. It takes the momentum deficit far downstream in the wake (Squire–Young formula) and defines $C_{d,p} = C_d - C_{d,f}$. Integrating $C_p$ around the surface for drag is numerically delicate: the result is a small difference of large contributions from the front and the back of the airfoil, so small errors in $C_p$ near the trailing edge have a large effect. The same applies to CFD results. Wake surveys or control-volume methods usually give more reliable drag than surface pressure integration.

The last row shows that skin friction contributes almost nothing to lift.

Reading the plots¶

For the default case (NACA 0012, $Re = 10^6$, $\alpha = 4^\circ$):

  • Stagnation point. It sits slightly below the leading edge on the lower surface, because the airfoil is at a positive angle of attack. There $C_p = 1$ and $C_f = 0$.
  • Suction peak. On the upper surface the flow accelerates sharply around the leading edge, giving a strong negative $C_p$ peak. Behind the peak, the pressure recovers towards the trailing edge, which is an adverse pressure gradient.
  • Upper-surface transition. The laminar boundary layer runs into this adverse gradient and weakens: $C_f$ drops almost to zero. Transition then happens in the first part of the chord, and $C_f$ jumps up from near the laminar curve towards the turbulent one.
  • Lower-surface transition. The pressure gradient on the lower surface stays mild over most of the chord, so the boundary layer remains laminar much longer. Near the trailing edge it cannot withstand the final pressure recovery and separates: the short region with $C_f < 0$ just upstream of the lower transition point is a laminar separation bubble. Transition in the separated shear layer makes the flow turbulent and reattach.
  • Lift. The area between the two $C_p$ curves is concentrated in the front part of the airfoil, which is why the aerodynamic centre of a thin airfoil is near the quarter chord.

You can change the parameters in Section 5 and re-run the notebook. For example, increase $\alpha$ to watch the suction peak grow and upper-surface transition move forward, or lower $Re$ to see larger laminar regions.
